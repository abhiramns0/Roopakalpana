Gesture-Based Drone Swarm Control in VR

Gesture library and detailed interactions

An end-to-end design of a virtual reality interface that lets users command and coordinate a swarm of drones through natural hand gestures. Starting with an elicitation study to capture intuitive gesture metaphors, I crafted a robust gesture library and iteratively prototyped the VR environment in Unity. Rigorous user testing informed refinements in interaction mechanics and feedback visuals, resulting in a seamless, immersive control system that improves task efficiency and user engagement.

Research

Gesture-based interfaces have gained significant attention due to their potential to simplify the complexities of swarm control.

A variety of approaches have been explored, yet gaps remain in creating a standardized and user-centered gesture library suitable for diverse and dynamic application areas.

Previous works

These works reinforce the need for intuitive interfaces but often rely on predefined gesture sets, limiting their adaptability across broader contexts.

The need for a standardized gesture library is evident. Octavian et al. (2023) also point to a lack of systematic efforts to develop a universal and adaptable gesture set — a gap this project aims to address.

Aerial swarm

Schranz et al. (2020) catalogue swarm robotic applications and behaviors across terrestrial, aerial and aquatic environments. Aerial swarms are difficult to test in real settings, which makes VR a good research bed.

Basic swarm behaviors: pattern formation, collective exploration, coordinated motion, task allocation and aggregation.

Swarm control architecture

Execution: low-level flight control such as collision avoidance and velocity regulation, which our system automates to offload operator burden.

Coordination: high-level grouping and task assignment.
Decision-making: path planning and formation commands.

Source: Schranz et al., Front. Robot. AI 7, 36 (2020)

Elicitation study

Insights came from military personnel and people working in rescue areas: reconnaissance, grenade drop, evacuation, post-disaster surveillance and more.

Considering real-life scenarios in both application areas, 18 actions were selected for the study.

Research bed

The setup built in Unity VR can be used for further studies in aerial swarm control through gestures. More studies can expand the library and test it in VR, gaining insights without the losses of real-world testing.

Final prototype

A fully interactive VR interface overlays a 3D terrain map and building X-ray vision, enabling operators to visualize structural interiors during emergencies. Users can dynamically group drones, assign each squad to distinct routes across the landscape, and adjust formation patterns on the fly, optimizing search-and-rescue missions and defense surveillance with intuitive gesture controls.
